How a repository functions is based largely on licensing the data it holds. The
establishing entity must be allowed to store and make the data available, and users must be
allowed to work with it. This leads to **two-way licensing**:

- the **deposition licence** — what a depositor grants the establishing entity (the founder),
  so the data can be stored and made available. Agreed at submission;

- the **user licence** — what a depositor grants users of the data, by choosing from what
  the repository offers. Decided per record.

## The deposition licence

A depositor grants the establishing entity the right to use the content — in particular to
store it and to make it available to the public. Consent is given by submitting the data,
in practice by ticking a consent box, with a link to the full text.

> In the interest of legal certainty, active consent should always be required. Terms
> placed somewhere in the repository are not enough on their own — in a dispute, the
> establishing entity would have to prove that the depositor had the opportunity to read
> them.

The depositor declares that they are entitled to grant the licence — that they hold all the
necessary rights, that they have the consent of all co-authors where relevant, or of an
employer where the data is a work made for hire, and that no third-party rights are
infringed. Where the data is a work made for hire, there is no generally valid answer to
who may grant the licence: it depends on how the institution regulates the exercise of
copyright. The depositor bears responsibility for the accuracy of the declaration.

### The NRP template

The NRP provides model deposition licence conditions. They may be **taken over in full**,
**adapted** to the needs of the establishing entity, or **replaced** by an arrangement in
the repository policy or in a consortium agreement.


- **Czech** — [PDF](https://www.eosc.cz/media/3853635/deposit_license_agreement_cs.pdf) · [DOCX](https://www.eosc.cz/media/3853634/deposit_license_agreement_cs.docx)

- **English** — [PDF](https://www.eosc.cz/media/3853637/deposit_license_agreement_en.pdf) · [DOCX](https://www.eosc.cz/media/3853636/deposit_license_agreement_en.docx)

The template deliberately does **not** cover the specific archival conditions of a repository, or the terms on which the establishing entity may remove data. **Those belong in the repository policy.**

> The document has no signature lines or fields identifying the depositor, and that is
> deliberate. Granting a non-exclusive licence does not require a written form, so consent
> is given by the act of submitting. The document was previously titled as a licence
> agreement; the name was changed because it raised questions about missing contractual
> particulars.

## The user licence

The user licence is what lets users work with the data. It is decided per record: the
depositor chooses from the licences the repository offers.

Where possible, **the repository should define the range of licences in advance** and let
the depositor choose between them. Where a range cannot be defined in advance, a depositor
can supply their own licence — an ad hoc licence.

Giving the depositor a choice between standardised **Creative Commons** licences is the
sensible solution.

Four things the repository has to ensure:

- **the licence is a required field** — the depositor chooses it as one of the required
  entries, and cannot leave it unset;

- **the chosen licence is visible** in the record, so that a user can easily find it;

- **it is stated in the metadata**, and in the README for datasets;

- **where a licence can be changed later**, the depositor is warned that the original one
  does not go away.

> Creative Commons licences are **not revocable** in practice. Once a work is published
> under one, users may use it under those terms. If a different licence is added later, the
> work ends up licensed under several licences at once.

## Creative Commons licences

Four elements, combined into six licences, plus CC0:

| Element | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **BY** | attribution — the name of the work, the author, the source and the licence must be stated. Present in every CC licence |
| **SA** | share-alike — a derivative work must carry the same licence |
| **NC** | non-commercial — the work may not be used for commercial purposes |
| **ND** | no derivatives — the work may not be modified, combined or otherwise processed |

| Licence | Derivatives | Commercial use |
|---|---|---|
| **CC BY** | yes | yes |
| **CC BY-SA** | yes, under the same licence | yes |
| **CC BY-NC** | yes | no |
| **CC BY-NC-SA** | yes, under the same licence | no |
| **CC BY-ND** | no | yes |
| **CC BY-NC-ND** | no | no |
| **CC0** | yes | yes — without any restriction on use |

**Version 4.0 is recommended** — it has no national adaptations and is the same in every
language, only translated. CC0 has a single version, CC0 1.0 Universal.

### Compatibility

When material under different licences is combined, three elements restrict what is
possible:

- **ND** — nothing. Material under ND cannot be combined or modified at all.

- **SA** — only material under the same or a compatible SA licence, since the result has to
  carry that licence.

- **NC** — nothing, if the result is to be used commercially.

### Three points specific to Czech law

**Copyright cannot be waived.** CC0 is written as a waiver — the author gives up their
rights and the work enters the public domain. Czech law does not allow that, so where CC0
is applied to a copyright work it is read as a licence without any restriction on use.

**A database is different.** While copyright is non-transferable and cannot be waived under
Czech law, the special database right can be waived and transferred. CC0 can therefore be
used to license a database.

**In repository practice, the distinction is not made.** CC0 and CC licences are applied
without determining which protection regime applies to the data.

## Software

CC licences are not suitable for software. They do not address the specifics of source code
or patents, and they are not compatible with software licences.

Software uses standardised **FOSS** licences, ranging from permissive to copyleft:

- **permissive** — such as **BSD** and **MIT**, which allow the code to be used and modified
  and not shared further, including in commercial projects;

- **copyleft** — such as **GNU GPL**, which requires further distribution of modified
  software to be under the same licence and the source code to be made available.

Where a project combines components under different licences, their licences have to be
compatible. The European Commission's licence tool and the **License Selector** developed
within the NRP can help with the choice.

## Licences in the metadata

Every dataset carries its licence, and it forms part of the metadata record. FAIR principle
**R1.1** requires exactly this: (Meta)data are released with a clear and accessible data
usage license.

**The licence terms have to be machine-readable** — intelligible to both people and
automated systems. Standardised formats are recommended:

- **CC REL** — Creative Commons Rights Expression Language

- **Dublin Core** — DC Terms

- **DCAT-AP**

- **JSON-LD**

- **ODRL** — Open Digital Rights Language

- **SPDX** — Software Package Data Exchange

ODRL and CC REL allow a more detailed description of the conditions under which a dataset
may be used. SPDX is widely supported in software ecosystems and is suitable where the
metadata relates to software components or open-source data files.

> Metadata exported to the National Metadata Directory **must** have a permissive licence
> that allows unrestricted harvesting and distribution — effectively equivalent to CC0.

**CC0 is the most common licence for metadata**, recommended together with CC BY. It
prevents *attribution stacking* — the need to state provenance at every further
redistribution — and gives the metadata set a single regime.

## What protects research data

There is no single system of legal protection for research data. Depending on the nature of
the data, different regimes apply; some data may be covered by several at once, and some by
none at all.

| Regime                     | Applies to                                                                                                                                                                                                 |
| -------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Copyright**              | data that are the result of creative activity — a photograph, a computer program, a database                                                                                                               |
| **Database protection**    | two kinds: copyright, where the selection or arrangement of elements is original; and the **special database right**, where obtaining, verifying or presenting the content involved substantial investment |
| **Personal data**          | under GDPR and the Act on the Processing of Personal Data                                                                                                                                                  |
| **Trade secrets**          | commercially significant facts; breach is an act of unfair competition                                                                                                                                     |
| **Classified information** | information marked with a degree of classification under the Act on the Protection of Classified Information                                                                                               |
| **Contractual protection** | arising from agreements such as a non-disclosure agreement                                                                                                                                                 |

The regimes do not exclude each other — data may be protected by copyright and be personal
data at the same time.

Some data are protected by none of these — for example values obtained by measurement with
meteorological instruments that do not form a database. These are **plain data**.

> **Research data cannot be owned.** There is no property right to them. A researcher has
> factual control over their data, and loses it at the moment of publication — but holds no
> legal title. Rights are usually held by the institution. A researcher moving between
> institutions cannot take the data along.

## Legal aspects

The terms under which a repository operates in the NRP are set out in binding documents:

- **[Terms and Conditions for operating repositories in the NRP](/en/docs/repo_admins/operating-repositories-in-the-nrp/formal-governing-documents)** — valid from 23 March 2026, in Czech and in English;

- **[Contract Proposal between the Repository Administrator and the Infrastructure](/en/docs/repo_admins/operating-repositories-in-the-nrp/formal-governing-documents)** — current version, in Czech.

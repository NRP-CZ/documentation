Not everything can be open. Even where research data should be as open as possible, ethical, legal and security limits mean that some of it cannot be — and the FAIR principles acknowledge this.

How the NRP treats sensitive data is set out in two documents: the **Charter of the EOSC CZ Working Group Sensitive Data** and the **Initial AAI architecture for sensitive data in NRP**. This page is an overview of both.

## What makes data sensitive

- they are intended strictly for a precisely defined group of people;

- they require special regulation because of their nature — protected by law, or by trade secrets, contracts or licences;

- access outside that group is likely to cause significant or damaging consequences — financial, moral, legal, or to health and life.

The category is decided by the **data owner**, unless the law says otherwise. In practical terms, ordinary protection measures are not enough, and the effort put into protection should be proportionate to the harm disclosure would cause.

## Levels of sensitivity

| Level | What it covers |
|---|---|
| **Low** | Public information and internal data — open research data, method descriptions, unpublished reports |
| **Medium** | Data under a contractual obligation of protection — donor or student records, intellectual property, contact information |
| **High** | Data protected by law or an equivalent principle — health, genetic and biometric data, trade secrets, data affecting state security |

## What it means for a repository

- **Access** is governed by agreements — data access request, data use agreement, data transfer agreement — and enforced through the AAI. Sensitive data require multi-factor authentication and may require identity vetting.
Whether a particular dataset needs a data access request, a data use agreement or a data transfer agreement — and what each of them has to contain — is set out in the **Initial AAI architecture for sensitive data in NRP**.
- **Processing** may have to happen inside a **trusted research environment**, not by downloading the data.

- **Metadata** — a record hidden at the metadata level cannot carry a DOI.

- **Policy** — the repository has to state the retention rules and the conditions for removal. Because of the nature of the data, these may differ from the general policy. → [Certification and Data Policy](/en/docs/repo_admins/general-topics/certification-and-data-policy)

## Where it is decided

Whether a repository will hold personal or otherwise sensitive data should be decided **before the repository is built**. It affects the metadata model, access control and storage.

## Personal data and GDPR

**Personal data** are any information relating to an identified or identifiable natural person — by reference to a name, an identification number, location data, an online identifier, or factors specific to their physical, physiological, genetic, mental, economic, cultural or social identity. They are protected under **GDPR** and the **Act on the Processing of Personal Data**.

Categories the Working Group treats as sensitive personal data: racial or ethnic origin, political opinions, religious or philosophical beliefs, trade union membership, genetic data, biometric data used to identify a person, data concerning health, and data concerning sex life or sexual orientation.

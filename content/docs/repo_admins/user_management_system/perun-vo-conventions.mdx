---
title: Conventions for NRP-related VOs
---

If you are a repository (or other NRP service) administrator, you surely
have a virtual organisation (VO) set up for you repository. The VO
typically handles access to the underlaying infrastructure, various
supporting services, and often to the repository itself.

## Motivation

Depending on the specifics of your repository implementation and
deployment, your roles can range from membership management of serveral
groups in the VO (usually when your repository is operated by the
infrastructure staff) to full management of the VO (when you operate the
service yourself).

For services the repository admins operate themselves, setup of the VO has
been thoroughly discussed when the service was established as it depends on
the particular situation.

For repositories operated mainly by the infrastructure, such as most of
CESNET-Invenio, CLARIN-DSpace, and ASEP/ARL instances, we tend to follow
conventions in the composition of groups and services in the VO to make it
more manageable.

## Groups in VOs for Standard Repositories

The recommended structure of the groups in typical VOs handling NRP
services usually contains following groups. Not all need to be present (and
the repository administrator may not have access to all of them), but
we strongly prefer sticking to the naming and purposes.

  - **repository_group_admins** is the group of people who have permissions
    to manage membership in other groups in this VO that handle services
    that need to be manned by the repository administrator. This group
    should be populated by the “higher technical management” of the
    repository.
  - **repository** and specifically its subgroups define roles of community
    members, curators, managers, etc.
  - **mailing_list** may be the mailing list for repository management
    and/or technical staff
  - **rt_queues** and its subgroups define personnel with access to the
    supporting queues [in the Request Tracker
    system](../user_support/user_support)
  - **infrastructure** and its subgroups define access to low-level storage
    and container deployments

Note that we heavily use the concept of membership relations/indirect
membership to populate those groups: it is possible to pre-fill members of
a group from another group. Those are listed with an icon of a person with
a double arrow indicating indirect membership.

For example, it makes little sense to have an RT queue watchers (i.e. those
who get mail from the user support system) who do not have access to
managing their support system queue through the web interface. We typically
set up the group managing web access to be filled indirectly with all users
in the appropriate group of watchers. So if you want somebody to receive
mails and to have access to the web, add them to the watchers group. If
somebody needs to have access but doesn't want to get mail, then to the
basic group. You don't have to add users to both groups.

Another example, if your service is very simple and you have just one group
of admins handling all the tasks, we would set up all the groups above and
reference those indirectly to your initial group of admins.

Note that virtual organisations and groups for standard repositories are
set up by the infrastructure staff for you.
